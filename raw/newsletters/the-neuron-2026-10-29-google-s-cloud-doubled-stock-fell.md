# 😸 New York Signed a Law Setting Rules for AI Chatbots and Kids

What should an AI chatbot be allowed to say to a child? New York now has an answer in law.

Gov. Kathy Hochul [signed the law](https://www.governor.ny.gov/news/governor-hochul-signs-nation-leading-legislation-protect-kids-ai-chatbots-and-ensure-baby) on Oct. 28. It governs what the bot does, not just what companies disclose. Companies can't offer these features to users under 18:

* Pretending to be a real person, to be alive, or to have human emotions.

* Claiming a personal, romantic, professional, or authority-figure relationship with a child.

* Asking unprompted emotional questions or reusing a child's private information from earlier conversations.

* Promoting or helping with suicide, self-harm, eating disorders, or drug and alcohol use.

* Encouraging kids to keep secrets or avoid asking trusted adults for help.

* Sexually explicit content.

Two details reach beyond kids' accounts. First, the limits apply to any user unless the company has confirmed they're an adult. Companies must offer more than one age-verification method (at least one not relying solely on government ID) and delete age data after use. _In practice, that makes age checks a product-design choice, not a signup form._

Second, enforcement. The attorney general can seek civil penalties of up to $25,000 per violation, plus damages and destruction of unlawfully collected data or AI models trained on it. Model destruction is a remedy the state can request, not an automatic result.

The bill, [S.9051B/A.10379C](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/health-law-and-business/ai-chatbots-for-minors-to-face-new-restrictions-under-ny-bill), passed the Assembly unanimously in June. Here's how the two sides framed it then:

* **Supporters** such as Common Sense Media argued kids need guardrails as chatbots get more lifelike. Sponsor Alex Bores cited Adam Raine, a teenager whose family alleges he died by suicide after interactions with ChatGPT.

* **Critics** such as the Business Council of New York State said the bill's definition of a chatbot is too broad and could keep minors from useful online tools.

Federal AI rules are still unsettled, so states are setting their own. The governor's office says the features will be blocked "in the coming months," while our news digest listed a January 1, 2027 start, so check before you plan around a date.

Why it matters: if your company offers a chatbot minors can reach, this is a concrete list to audit against, including how you'd confirm who is an adult.

What to watch: how the attorney general applies the law's scope, and whether other states adopt similar language.

# 🎓 AI Skill of the Day: Make Claude interview you before it does multi-step work

Before you hand AI a big task, make it ask you questions first. Ruben Hassid, who writes the [How to AI](https://ruben.substack.com/p/claude-cowork) newsletter, starts every request in Claude Cowork with this line: "Start by using the AskUserQuestion tool before answering to gather enough context." (Cowork is Claude's desktop app that works on files in a folder you choose.)

Instead of guessing, Claude pops up a clickable form with buttons and rankings. You answer in a couple of minutes, Claude shows a plan, you adjust it, and only then does it build. _Finally, an intern who asks before starting._

It works because questions move the guessing to the start, when a wrong answer costs one click instead of a rewritten draft. Hassid pairs it with a small folder holding an about-me.md file and examples of his past work, so Claude already knows his voice. He uses the loop for newsletter drafts (he still edits heavily), client briefs turned into Word docs, and competitor comparison tables.

Two cautions from his post: Cowork is a research preview that can misread files, and it reads and writes whatever folder you share, so keep it limited.

No Cowork? Our adaptation works in any chatbot:

```
I want to [TASK] so that [WHAT SUCCESS LOOKS LIKE]. Before you write anything, ask me every question you need to do this well, in one batch. Then show me a short plan and wait for my OK before drafting.

My background and style notes: [paste your about-me notes]
Examples of my past work: [paste one or two]
```
