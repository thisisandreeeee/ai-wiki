# 😸** Who really wins from Anthropic’s record IPO?**

As you may have heard, Anthropic began trading on Nasdaq on Thursday, with its IPO priced at $83 a share. Each of its seven founders [sold roughly](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2130357/000162828026074944/anthropicpbc-424b4.htm) **$280 million in shares**, before fees and taxes.

And that was just the cash payout. **Each founder still held roughly $20 billion in Anthropic shares at the IPO price.** That wealth remains on paper and will rise or fall with the stock.

But a new name on the stock exchange doesn’t automatically mean the money is flowing to new places. Some of the biggest beneficiaries are companies you already know: Amazon and Google. They own large pieces of the company behind Claude. And they also [supply its computing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2130357/000162828026074944/anthropicpbc-424b4.htm) power, giving them two ways to benefit from its growth.

Here’s how the winnings stack up:

* The tech giants: [Amazon and Google’s](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2130357/000162828026074944/anthropicpbc-424b4.htm) disclosed holdings were worth roughly $161 billion and $147 billion at the offering price. Anthropic also [committed to spend](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2130357/000162828026074944/anthropicpbc-424b4.htm) at least $110 billion with Amazon from May 2026 through April 2036, generally regardless of usage.

* The venture investors: [Menlo](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/anthropics-15-trillion-debut-rewrites-venture-capitals-playbook-5040303) invested more than $1 billion for a stake reportedly worth above $20 billion at a $1.5 trillion valuation. [Lightspeed](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/anthropics-15-trillion-debut-rewrites-venture-capitals-playbook-5040303) invested over $3 billion and held an even bigger stake. [Spark](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/anthropics-15-trillion-debut-rewrites-venture-capitals-playbook-5040303) invested back in 2023, when the whole company was valued at just $4.5 billion. Buying in earlier meant paying much less for a piece of the company.

* The banks: Underwriters earned [$550 million](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2130357/000162828026074944/anthropicpbc-424b4.htm) in fees. _Nice work if you can underwrite it._

The founders also [pledged 80%](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2130357/000162828026074944/anthropicpbc-424b4.htm) of their personal equity to philanthropy, although those pledges aren’t legally binding.

**Follow the money.** Amazon and Google help fund a competitor that gives us more choice. But its success also drives growth in their investments and computing businesses. Their shares are [nonvoting](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2130357/000162828026074944/anthropicpbc-424b4.htm), so ownership doesn’t mean control. Still, this means more money flowing to the same tech giants.

_Your investor is also your landlord. And the minimum rent is $110 billion._

Other paydays could follow. [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/openai-submits-confidential-s-1/) has filed confidential IPO paperwork, and [Nscale](https://www.nscale.com/press-releases/nscale-files-initial-public-offering) has filed publicly. [Canva](https://www.capitalbrief.com/article/canva-steps-up-us-public-market-preparations-with-ipo-focused-hiring-push-7ef11b3f-e966-464f-aa2b-aa7f4a923c14/) and [Lambda](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/lambda-seeks-4bn-funding-round-ahead-of-planned-ipo-report/) have been linked to 2027 listings, while [Databricks](https://podcast.newcomer.co/episode/databricks-ceo-on-what-happens-to-ai-without-jensen-huang) says it will go public eventually. None has a confirmed listing date.

**What about us?** The investor payoff is visible, but the public benefit still has to be delivered. I’d watch whether the gains reach beyond shareholders through affordable tools, useful discoveries, and room for smaller companies to compete.

AI policy researcher [Molly Kinder](https://rooseveltinstitute.org/blog/policy-experts-discuss-what-anthropics-ipo-means-for-the-economy/) raises a broader concern: the workers whose jobs AI could disrupt aren’t the people positioned to share in its wealth. She argues for giving workers more say in how AI is used and ways to share the gains, including ownership stakes. Cheaper software alone won’t settle the issue when AI changes someone’s entire livelihood.

# 🎓 AI Skill of the Day: **Turn dashboards into slides with Gemini’s new Mac agent**

Google’s [new Mac agent](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/products/gemini-app/computer-use-mac/) can control your computer and handle tasks across apps and websites by clicking, typing, and navigating for you. Ask it to gather figures from a dashboard that tracks website traffic or sales and turn them into Google Slides. It works in a separate background window while you keep using your Mac.

Try it for your next weekly report. Open Gemini’s Mac app and give it a specific assignment:

```
Open [dashboard URL] and compare the past seven days with the previous seven. Ask which metrics matter before starting.

Build a five-slide Google Slides presentation:
1. Overall performance
2. The biggest gain
3. The biggest decline
4. Other notable changes
5. Questions worth investigating

Include screenshots and source links. Label the date ranges clearly. Don’t invent explanations for changes the data cannot explain. Save the deck as a draft for my review.
```
The useful habit: specify the finished deliverable, including what evidence belongs in it. Before sharing, check the numbers against the dashboard and confirm both periods use the same filters.

Rolling out in U.S. English for Google AI Plus, Pro, and Ultra subscribers with a personal Google account.
