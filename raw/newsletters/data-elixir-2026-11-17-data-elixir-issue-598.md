​Same Person, Five Records ( https://click.dataelixir.com/75ume209vku8h8kk74dbzhwwdxep7tnh838om/z2hghnhegdpk5mfph0/aHR0cHM6Ly9tLWNsYXJrLmdpdGh1Yi5pby9wb3N0cy8yMDI2LTExLTA1LWlkZW50aXR5LXJlc29sdXRpb24vP3V0bV9jYW1wYWlnbj1EYXRhX0VsaXhpciZ1dG1fc291cmNlPURhdGFfRWxpeGlyXzU5OA== )​
**********************************************************************************************************************************************************************************************************************************************************************************

Matching records that refer to the same person sounds straightforward until you're dealing with millions of messy records. This case study covers probabilistic matching, data quality problems, and evaluating results without reliable ground truth. It also shows where LLMs can help and where traditional statistical methods still have the advantage.
​Michael Clark

*******************************************************************************************************************************************************************************************************************************************************
​When Equal Survival Proves Nothing ( https://click.dataelixir.com/75ume209vku8h8kk74dbzhwwdxep7tnh838om/p8heh9h4v7g2refqh3/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cuZmhhcnJlbGwuY29tL3Bvc3QvdHJ1bmMvP3V0bV9jYW1wYWlnbj1EYXRhX0VsaXhpciZ1dG1fc291cmNlPURhdGFfRWxpeGlyXzU5OA== )​
*******************************************************************************************************************************************************************************************************************************************************

Even when your sanity checks pass, your analysis can still be wrong. In a simulated trial where treatment doesn't do anything, survival is equal across both arms, but comparing only the survivors shows a highly significant effect. This post shows how selection bias can creep in whenever you analyze only who's left after treatment.
​Statistical Thinking | Frank Harrell

****************************************************************************************************************************************************************************************************************************************************************************************************
​Anatomy of Apple's Personal Knowledge Graph ( https://click.dataelixir.com/75ume209vku8h8kk74dbzhwwdxep7tnh838om/x0hph6heg79pkgc5hl/aHR0cHM6Ly84cmFuay5haS9nYW1lL2FwcGxlLWludGVsbGlnZW5jZS1wZXJzb25hbC1rbm93bGVkZ2UtZ3JhcGg_dXRtX2NhbXBhaWduPURhdGFfRWxpeGlyJnV0bV9zb3VyY2U9RGF0YV9FbGl4aXJfNTk4 )​
****************************************************************************************************************************************************************************************************************************************************************************************************

Your Mac thinks it knows who your family is. It decides with an XGBoost classifier fed 144 features, like how often someone shows up in your photos on Saturday nights. This deep dive maps what Apple’s on-device graph stores, infers, and keeps even after you delete it. There's an open-source tool here too so you can see your own graph.
​The Eighth Rank | Dario Pasquini and Michał Bazyli

*************************************************************************************************************************************************************************************************************************************************************************************
​A Worked Example ( https://click.dataelixir.com/75ume209vku8h8kk74dbzhwwdxep7tnh838om/6qheh8hlq2n5zvsohk/aHR0cHM6Ly9jbHN0YWN5LmdpdGh1Yi5pby9wb3N0cy8yMDI2LzExL3dvcmtlZC1leGFtcGxlLWhvbmVzdC1vbWljcy1tb2RlbC8_dXRtX2NhbXBhaWduPURhdGFfRWxpeGlyJnV0bV9zb3VyY2U9RGF0YV9FbGl4aXJfNTk4 )​
*************************************************************************************************************************************************************************************************************************************************************************************

On this dataset the popular pipeline reports an AUC of 0.89. But the honest number is 0.59, and its probabilities are so overconfident that it does worse than predicting “24%” for everyone. Most of the gap comes from selecting features outside validation. This worked example shows exactly where inflated results come from.
​Carson Stacy

​smooth in Python: TBATS implementation ( https://click.dataelixir.com/75ume209vku8h8kk74dbzhwwdxep7tnh838om/25h2hoh35o486gs3h4/aHR0cHM6Ly9vcGVuZm9yZWNhc3Qub3JnLzIwMjYvMTEvMTYvc21vb3RoLWluLXB5dGhvbi10YmF0cy1pbXBsZW1lbnRhdGlvbi8_dXRtX2NhbXBhaWduPURhdGFfRWxpeGlyJnV0bV9zb3VyY2U9RGF0YV9FbGl4aXJfNTk4 )​

Forecasting gets tricky when data has multiple seasonal patterns, missing values, and effects from promotions or prices. This new TBATS implementation handles all of that and fixes a quirk in the original model, where ARMA errors feed into the level and can amplify shocks instead of letting them fade. Early benchmarks look promising.
​OpenForecast | Ivan Svetunkov
