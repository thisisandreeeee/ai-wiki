### [7 Statistical Rules of Thumb: How Good Are They?](https://statisticsbyjim.com/basics/7-statistical-rules-of-thumb/?utm_campaign=Data_Elixir&utm_source=Data_Elixir)

Statistical rules of thumb are easy to remember but for them to be useful, you need to know when to trust them. In this post, Jim Frost walks through seven familiar shortcuts, including the rule of 30 and the 80/20 principle, explaining where they come from, when they help, and how much confidence they deserve.
_Jim Frost_


--------------------
### [A DuckDB Database with No Data in It](https://duckdb.org/2026/10/07/view-only-mode?utm_campaign=Data_Elixir&utm_source=Data_Elixir_593)

A DuckDB database doesn't need to hold any data to be useful. This walkthrough shows how to store a database of views that point to remote Parquet files, which essentially turns it into a tiny, shared catalog. Users get ready-to-query datasets through a single URL, while the data stays where it is.
_The DuckDB team_


--------------------
### [How TABLESAMPLE picks rows](https://boringsql.com/posts/tablesample/?utm_campaign=Data_Elixir&utm_source=Data_Elixir_593)

Sampling can make a query much faster, but it matters how you pick the rows. In this post, Radim Marek explains PostgreSQL’s sampling options, why nearby rows can give you less information than you expect, and how to build consistent samples that still work across related tables.
_Radim Marek_


--------------------
### [Benchmarking the Future of Work](https://www.mercor.com/blog/human-baselines-for-benchmarks-ai-now-outperforms-junior-accountants/?utm_source=dataelixir&utm_medium=newsletter&utm_campaign=apex&utm_content=10_12)

AI can now outperform licensed accountants on certain detailed accounting tasks, but that doesn’t mean it can do the whole job. Using tasks adapted from APEX-Accounting, Mercor explores what these results suggest about the future of work and the growing importance of communication, context, and asking the right questions.
_// sponsored_

# Data Visualization


--------------------
### [Tufte's Razor - A guide to the data-ink ratio](https://tuftesrazor.scienceux.org/?utm_campaign=Data_Elixir&utm_source=Data_Elixir_593)

Edward Tufte’s data-ink ratio encourages people to remove chart elements that don’t convey information, but how far should you go? This interactive guide lets you adjust bars, labels, and other details to see what makes a chart more clear and what gets lost along the way.
_Science UX | Tufte's Razor_


--------------------
### [ggindex](https://discindo.github.io/ggindex/?utm_campaign=Data_Elixir&utm_source=Data_Elixir_593)

Sometimes a few points on a chart need more explanation than a short label can provide. ggindex is a new ggplot2 extension that marks those points with numbers and puts their descriptions in a matching legend. That gives you room to explain them without crowding the chart.
_Discindo_

### [Release of Polars 2.0](https://pola.rs/posts/release-polars-2/?utm_campaign=Data_Elixir&utm_source=Data_Elixir_593)

Polars 2.0 brings some useful changes for everyday data work, including streaming by default, the ability to spill some operations to disk when memory runs short, and expanded SQL support. If you’re upgrading, check the migration guide, especially if your analysis depends on row order.
_Polars | Ritchie Vink_

### [An explosion of R package and environment managers](https://rworks.dev/posts/r-managers/?utm_campaign=Data_Elixir&utm_source=Data_Elixir_593)

R has several new tools for managing packages and environments and it can be hard to keep them straight. In this post, Isabella Velásquez explains how five options differ, with examples for sharing a project, recreating its software environment, or making a single script easier to rerun.
_R Works | Isabella Velásquez_
