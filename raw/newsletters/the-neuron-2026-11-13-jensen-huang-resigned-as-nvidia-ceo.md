# 😸 Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang resigns after 33 years, and his CFO steps in

[Jensen Huang resigned](https://apnews.com/article/f9ac67fed10c86c1ed1ad1f41dd921a5) as Nvidia's CEO on Thursday, effective immediately, and left the board too. Nvidia's [SEC filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001045810/000104581026000082/nvidiaannouncesceotransiti.htm) says CFO Colette Kress is now interim CEO while the board searches for a permanent one. Director Harvey Jones becomes the board's first-ever chair.

Huang co-founded Nvidia in 1993, so this is the first CEO change in its history. _Thirty-three years, one CEO, and four days from first admission to exit._ Nvidia hasn't given a reason.

Here's what happened:

* Nov 8: Huang told employees he'd been unfaithful to his wife, and the board [opened an independent investigation](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/statement-from-nvidia-board-of-directors) into reports he was targeted by a foreign intelligence effort.

* Nov 11: The Wall Street Journal reported the board was preparing to remove him, and the filing says it accepted his resignation that day.

* Nov 12: Nvidia announced it, shares fell more than 13% in early trading, and Huang agreed to testify before Congress, per [TechCrunch's timeline](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/12/nvidia-ceo-jensen-huangs-affair-with-alleged-chinese-spy-a-timeline/).

Why a national security story? New York Times reporting says U.S. officials believe the woman works for Chinese intelligence. She's also reportedly an executive at Megaspeed, a cloud company investigated over whether it helped Chinese firms get around chip export controls.

Two caveats. The board says it has no reason to believe national security or company secrets were compromised, and her alleged spy status isn't officially confirmed. Still, governance professors told TechCrunch they know of no precedent for a CEO caught up in a suspected honeypot, which is spy-speak for a relationship used to get close to someone with influence. _Not a sentence we planned to write today._

U.S. officials say [separate Justice Department investigations](https://www.semafor.com/article/11/12/2026/us-doj-probes-nvidia) involving Nvidia, Megaspeed, and Supermicro cover potential antitrust and chip-smuggling issues. [The Atlantic argues](https://www.theatlantic.com/technology/2026/11/jensen-huangs-icarus-complex/689393/) the damage could spread because Nvidia sells chips to AI labs and also helps financially back some of them, so one shaky link could wobble the whole loop. That's an argument, not a forecast.

**Why this matters: **Our own Corey Noles [thinks](https://www.theneuron.ai/news/jensen-huang-resigns-nvidia-future/) Nvidia will probably be fine long term, but the transition could be rough. His case: a CEO can leave in a day, while the ecosystem built around his company can't.

The big piece is CUDA, Nvidia's software layer that lets developers write programs for its chips. Nvidia's 2026 annual report says more than 7.5M developers use it. Picture a finance team living in one spreadsheet program. Switching means rewriting every formula and retraining everyone mid-close. _Translation: the boss left, the plumbing didn't._

The slower risk is customers who keep buying today while planning to depend on Nvidia less tomorrow. Corey keeps the business question separate from the conduct one, and calls what Huang admitted unacceptable. His watch list: product delivery, leadership retention, licensing terms, and the pace of Nvidia's investments.

The first real test is Tuesday, November 17, when Nvidia reports quarterly earnings with Kress as interim CEO. Listen for whether orders hold, what she says about China, and any hint of a permanent pick.

# 🎓 AI Skill of the Day: Let a big model plan and Gemini 4 Hydrogen do the work

Google's [Gemini 4 Hydrogen launch post](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/introducing-gemini-4-hydrogen/) describes it as the fast, lower-cost model for everyday coding and agent work, built to execute quickly rather than reason deeply. The heavier Gemini 4 models, Carbon and Argon, take the long, hard thinking. Google's own coding tool, Antigravity, already splits the job that way. Its Orchestrator switches between Carbon and Hydrogen in one session, and Google reports near-Carbon results with about 24% less quota and 19% faster completion on its own coding tests. _Vendor numbers, so season to taste._

The post gives no prompting advice, so here's our version of the pattern for any chat. Think architect and contractor. The architect decides what to build and in what order. The contractor follows the blueprint quickly. Paying the architect to hammer every nail is slow and pricey, and asking the contractor to design the building is how you get a door that opens into a wall.

Run it in four moves:

1. Plan with your strongest model. Ask it to turn the job into numbered steps, each written to stand alone, with a "done looks like" check you can verify in 30 seconds.

2. Run one step at a time on Hydrogen. Pick it in the Gemini app's model dropdown (free users may get it through auto model selection instead). Paste the full plan for context, then tell it to do only step 3. Google says it follows tone, format, and specific rules closely, which is what you want here.

3. Match the thinking depth. Hydrogen has four thinking levels: low, medium, high (the default), and xhigh (new). Google says you pay and wait only for the depth a task needs, so if your setup lets you choose, save xhigh for the one tricky step.

4. Send only the failures back upstairs. If a step misses its check, hand that step and its output back to the big model.

Here's the planning prompt:

```
I'm going to hand this job to a fast model one step at a time, so write the plan for it.

Job: [describe the job and what finished looks like]

Break it into numbered steps. For each step, give me:
- an instruction that makes sense on its own
- the input it needs from earlier steps
- a one-line "done looks like" check

Mark any step that needs real judgment, not just execution.
```
The thing to test is Google's speed claim. Google measured Hydrogen's 2.5x speedup at its high default setting against an older model, Gemini 3.8 Flash. So time one of your own tasks at a lower thinking level before deciding where Hydrogen earns its keep.
