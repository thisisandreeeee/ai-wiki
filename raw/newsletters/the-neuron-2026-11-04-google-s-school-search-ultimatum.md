# 😸 Google Is Giving Schools an AI Off Switch, and Search Goes Down With It

How do you keep AI out of a classroom without also taking away Google? That's the puzzle Google just handed to every school IT department. On Tuesday, Google [announced new controls](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/education/ai-learning-era-updates-2026/) saying Gemini, Gemini Notebook, Gemini in Classroom, and Google Search will all be off by default for K-12 student accounts until a school administrator turns them on.

Here's the catch. Google's education VP, Chris Phillips, told The New York Times that Search and AI are deeply integrated. That means [students at schools that ban AI would lose Search](https://gizmodo.com/google-is-holding-its-search-engine-hostage-from-schools-that-dont-want-ai-2000834544) and see a school message saying it's unavailable. _Time to dust off the card catalog._

Why this lands now: it answers a complaint from July. Common Sense Media [rated Google's AI Search an unacceptable risk](https://thenextweb.com/news/google-ai-search-unsafe-schools-common-sense-media) for young users, pointing out that schools and parents couldn't switch off the AI and keep Search. They could only block Google entirely. Google called the testing narrow and ambiguous. The group had tested consumer accounts rather than school ones, though Google confirmed the AI experience is the same for everyone. _So the fix looks like a cleaner version of the same trade-off, now with an official button._

Here's what Google announced, [per Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2305432/google-says-that-students-in-schools-that-ban-ai-wont-have-access-to-search/):

* Gemini, Gemini Notebook, Gemini in Classroom, and Search start off for K-12 student accounts, and Google says admins can enable each product separately.

* The controls roll out over the coming months, and Google says it will brief schools first.

* A Guided Learning pilot in Search starts this month at select U.S. schools. When Search spots a homework request, it walks the student through the problem step by step instead of handing over the answer.

* Guided Learning is already available worldwide in Google Classroom, where teachers can assign it. In the school version, teachers can see transcripts of students' chatbot conversations.

Google is also under pressure. In August, it switched on some AI features by default in Classroom, educators pushed back, and Google reversed course. In September, New York City and Los Angeles, the two largest school districts, restricted student access to generative AI on school devices. In October, Senators Richard Blumenthal and Josh Hawley wrote to CEO Sundar Pichai asking for Google's research on potential harms to children.

One thing is still fuzzy. Google says admins can enable each product separately, yet Phillips says Search and AI are tied together. Until Google publishes the actual settings, we won't know whether a school can leave Gemini off and keep Search on.

Here's what we'd watch. That exact setting is what Common Sense said was missing in July, and it's the difference between a real choice and a bundle. Engadget notes that districts rejecting Google's rules may end up weighing other search engines and devices.

# 🎓 AI Skill of the Day: Sort your agent's actions by what happens if it's wrong

Here's the scene that opens Hassann's [guide to agent guardrails](https://dev.to/hassann/ai-agent-guardrails-approval-gates-and-blast-radius-control-23hl). An AI agent (software that takes actions for you) is working through support tickets at 3 AM, decides one is an escalation, and emails an accurate summary to the user's boss. Nobody asked for it, and nothing could stop it. _The agent stayed inside its instructions, which is the scary part._

The problem was that nothing sat between the agent's decision and the send. Hassann's fix is to sort every action by what happens if the agent gets it wrong. Reads and searches run on their own. Sends, deletes, and payments get an approval gate, meaning the agent shows you the exact request before anything happens. His test: if the agent got this wrong 100 times, would that be acceptable? If not, gate it.

Try our version on any agent you run:

```
1. Safe and reversible (reads, searches, drafts): let it run.
2. Hard to undo (sends, deletes, payments): require my approval.
3. Not sure: treat it as number 2.
4. Cap how many number 2 actions it can take per hour.
```
The full guide adds dry-run mode (the agent shows what it would do without doing it) and ways to test your gates.
