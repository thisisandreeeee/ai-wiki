# 🙀 The “AI Data Center Revolt” Is Actually a REALLY big deal for the AI industry…

View image: (https://media.beehiiv.com/cdn-cgi/image/fit=scale-down,format=auto,onerror=redirect,quality=80/uploads/asset/file/5a2ca3c7-1764-4cc6-a9ac-1e1becfa2a50/Screenshot_2026-08-05_at_8.19.29_PM.png?t=1785986392)
Follow image link: (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rbgvTlt1VB8)
Caption: Going long on this one today, but it’s important. LENGTH FOR EMPHASIS! 

The data-center revolt may be **the year's biggest AI story**: Americans who disagree on everything agree _they hate datacenters._ Reporter Jasmine Sun shared lessons from a [10-day, four-site Wisconsin and Michigan trip](https://jasmi.news/p/no-data-centers-in-my-backyard?utm_source=chatgpt.com) on [The Ezra Klein Show](https://youtu.be/rbgvTlt1VB8?si=syi6bgoam0NPc2zO).

* [Opposition is bipartisan](https://youtu.be/rbgvTlt1VB8?si=syi6bgoam0NPc2zO&t=0): roughly 70% oppose a nearby center; 100+ moratorium proposals circulate.

* [Complaints](https://youtu.be/rbgvTlt1VB8?si=syi6bgoam0NPc2zO&t=515): huge power demand, noise, and _very_ ugly buildings.

* [Power](https://youtu.be/rbgvTlt1VB8?si=syi6bgoam0NPc2zO&t=584): projects outrun grids, raising rates and rebuilding public infrastructure for one wealthy customer.

* [Water](https://youtu.be/rbgvTlt1VB8?si=syi6bgoam0NPc2zO&t=522): newer closed-loop systems recycle most, but scarcity symbolizes who gets resources.

* [Secret NDAs](https://youtu.be/rbgvTlt1VB8?si=syi6bgoam0NPc2zO&t=395) fueled distrust.

* [Residents distrust promised jobs, taxes, and rates](https://youtu.be/rbgvTlt1VB8?si=syi6bgoam0NPc2zO&t=723): _"I don't believe them."_

* [AI lacks a constituency](https://youtu.be/rbgvTlt1VB8?si=syi6bgoam0NPc2zO&t=1273). Housing has residents, auto plants workers, renewables environmentalists; AI feels useful, not town-transforming essential.

* ["Permanent underclass" fears](https://youtu.be/rbgvTlt1VB8?si=syi6bgoam0NPc2zO&t=1877) ask residents to supply resources for infrastructure that may make them disposable. _NO thanks._

Residents face noise, construction, power lines, possible rate hikes, few permanent jobs, tax breaks, and private decisions. _These are real local conversations._ Daily ChatGPT users can still reject the idea that enjoying an app means their town owes the AI industry ("a handful of billionaires") land, cheap resources, and political deference. Before US midterms, AI is kitchen-table politics: bills, farmland, jobs, democracy, and whether towns can say no (_ish?_).

Sun says [some deals help](https://youtu.be/rbgvTlt1VB8?si=syi6bgoam0NPc2zO&t=3066): a developer might spend $30M cleaning contaminated land and reviving factories, if towns transparently share the upside. Moratoriums won't stop centers; they'll move overseas, out of state, or _into space._ [Compute is geopolitical leverage](https://youtu.be/rbgvTlt1VB8?si=syi6bgoam0NPc2zO&t=3248) for frontier models and cybersecurity, so authoritarian hosts could reduce US control. The [AI Futures pacing plan](https://blog.aifutures.org/p/how-to-pace-the-us-frontier) instead makes labs reserve most compute for existing models and safety tests, turning infrastructure into government's AI throttle.

Jasmine's sharpest point: industry calls this marketing when **_nah, it's a PRODUCT problem; AI is problematic._** AI feels like a toy or boost, not $1T+ infrastructure. We're scaling before fixing alignment, hallucinations, context/memory, continual learning, and efficiency. IMO one architecture causes all five; more centers won't fix it. It is _literally_ unsustainable. So candidates must answer: "**Who pays for AI, who gets to decide where it's built, and who gets the benefits?**"

# 🎓 AI Skill of the Day: Build an Agent Knowledge Flywheel

AI can do great work and forget the lesson. [Yisong Yue's "knowledge flywheel"](https://x.com/yisongyue/status/2085043769297277114) makes agent runs reusable memory.

Save what worked, failed, when, and why in instructions or shared knowledge the next agent reads.

After substantial work:

1. Review the run.

2. Extract lessons that change the next similar task.

3. Save them for the next session.

**Favorite insight:** the system can remember even if the model doesn't.

```
Review the task we just completed. Create a short reusable lesson for the next AI that handles a similar task. Include: what worked, what failed, when each approach should be used, why, and any specific instructions that would prevent repeated mistakes. Keep only information that would materially improve the next run.
```
