# 😺 a16z maps AI’s shift from software to infrastructure

View image: (https://media.beehiiv.com/cdn-cgi/image/fit=scale-down,format=auto,onerror=redirect,quality=80/uploads/asset/file/84b034a9-b1e1-4ccf-b630-dc4e41b110ea/Screenshot_2026-10-04_at_8.31.46_AM.png?t=1791127917)

**DEEP DIVE: **[Read our full breakdown on the a16z report](https://www.theneuron.ai/news/a16z-state-of-markets-2026-ai-became-a-capital-cycle/)

And today, in total market concentration overload: Tech accounted for _roughly 76% _of S&P 500 earnings growth in 2026 through late August, according to venture firm [a16z’s latest markets report](https://a16z.com/state-of-markets-ii/). _It’s a great read, full of loads of spicy charts!_

**Our takeaway:** AI is changing who gets paid. The biggest tech companies are pouring profits into chips, electricity, and networking equipment. And their spending becomes someone else’s sales.

**Here’s how big the buildout has become:**

* Alphabet, Amazon, Meta, Microsoft, and Oracle spent $416B on capital investment in 2025, per the report’s [spending chart](https://d1lamhf6l6yk6d.cloudfront.net/uploads/2026/10/State-of-Markets-Sep-2026.pdf#page=15).

* July analyst estimates put 2026 spending at $777B and f**orecast $1.1T** for 2027.

* Major cloud providers’ revenue backlogs more than doubled year over year: customer commitments that haven’t yet become recognized revenue.

Those totals cover capital investment, not AI spending alone. _“We’re investing in AI” is papering over a lot of details in those budget meetings TBH._

**Important fact: **Expanding AI capacity takes lots of equipment and electricity. Big tech profits have funded much of the buildout; now [borrowing is funding more](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-02/broadcom-starts-amassing-60-billion-to-fund-chips-for-anthropic). Plus, manufacturing investment, electrification, and defense spending add demand beyond AI.

Older equipment is staying useful, too. NVIDIA’s older (_feel like eons old in AI years) _A100 chip rental prices **remained at or above** their start-of-year levels, [a16z says](https://a16z.com/state-of-markets-ii/). Newer chips arrived, but customers still wanted the old ones’ computing power.

Cheaper AI models to run can make that appetite bigger too. Lower costs make more tasks easier to automate. a16z argues those efficiency gains expand usage rather than simply shrink bills. [_Jevon’s paradox_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jevons_paradox)_, folks. Internalize it._

**Why this matters:** **The buildout is easier to measure than the payoff.** Nearly 30% of S&P 500 companies reported “quantifiable impact” from AI, but only about 2% [disclosed a metric they track over time](https://d1lamhf6l6yk6d.cloudfront.net/uploads/2026/10/State-of-Markets-Sep-2026.pdf#page=27). Without that, outsiders can’t tell whether the reported gains persist or grow, even if companies measure them internally. Separately, only about 2% of U.S. households paid for AI services in April.

Software, meanwhile, faces a growth problem. About 75% of public software companies were profitable, but only around 30% were growing 20% or more. _For my finance nerds in the chat, you know this one:_ With higher interest rates, investors are less willing to pay high-growth prices for slower-growing businesses.

**Our take:** a16z sees robotics, biotechnology, and healthcare driving the next wave of AI demand. If that bet holds, AI investment will need to flow into factories, biotech labs, and hospital systems in addition to data centers.

So we gotta ask ourselves: Can a nurse spend less time on paperwork? Can a lab test a promising treatment faster? Can a robot take over a dangerous job without creating a new one for whoever supervises it? 

That’s the next flex: what all this spending actually gets people. _Preferably something besides another subscription._

_Personally, we’re struck by the recent observation that AI would actually make a lot of gains in automated material science vs automated biology science because you don’t need those pesky human trials in the middle. The bottleneck there is actually good material science data. Who’s building THAT? _

# 🎓 AI Skill of the Day: Get AI to explain it a different way

AI gave you an explanation. You read it twice. Still nothing. [Andrej Karpathy shared four ways](https://x.com/karpathy/status/2105819303471976479) to make model output easier to understand, starting with a writing trick he’s found useful and moving beyond text. _Another six paragraphs may not be the cure._

* **Make the language simpler**. Ask for an explanation using [ASD-STE100](https://www.asd-ste100.org/), an English standard made for aerospace maintenance documents. Karpathy sometimes requests “80% of the way to ASD-STE100” to loosen its strict rules while keeping the writing readable.

* **Ask for a diagram. **Have the model draw what it’s explaining instead of describing everything in prose. This can make relationships easier to follow.

* **Turn it into a webpage.** In a coding-capable AI tool, request the explanation “in HTML,” the format browsers display. Karpathy suggests interactive pages and animations so you can explore the explanation.

* **Try a narrated video.** Ask for a visual explainer like [3Blue1Brown’s](https://www.3blue1brown.com/), with [ElevenLabs](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/overview/intro) narration. Connecting ElevenLabs requires an API key (an access credential); Karpathy also suggests asking for free alternatives that run on your computer.

**Our suggestion**: take one explanation that lost you and ask for a diagram first. Check it against the original. The real test is whether you can explain the idea on your own, not just whether the output looks impressive.
