# 😺 Zuckerberg Just Publicly Broke Ranks With OpenAI and Anthropic On Slowing Down AI

Yesterday we told you that [Sam Altman and Dario Amodei](https://www.pacingthefrontier.com/) backed a petition asking Washington to build the tools to "pace" AI development. Well, [Mark Zuckerberg](https://www.wsj.com/tech/ai/mark-zuckerberg-says-u-s-should-accelerate-ai-development-not-restrict-it-3bbe0868) took out a Wall Street Journal op-ed to go out on his own and say: hard pass.

**Here's what happened:**

* Zuckerberg argued the U.S. "should accelerate AI development, not restrict it," and said the real risk isn't AI moving too fast, it's "superintelligence" (AI that outperforms humans at most tasks) ending up locked inside a handful of labs.

* He said distributing powerful AI broadly would create _more_ jobs and make it easier to start a business without raising huge amounts of money, not fewer jobs like critics fear.

* He also pushed back on banning Chinese open-weight AI models (models anyone can download and modify for free), calling it unlikely to actually work.

* The op-ed landed the same week OpenAI and Anthropic [officially endorsed](https://www.semafor.com/article/07/29/2026/openai-anthropic-back-calls-for-us-to-pace-tech-development) a petition from 1,224 frontier-lab employees asking governments to build "the technical and governance tools" to slow AI down if it starts improving itself faster than anyone can supervise.

**Why this matters:** This is the clearest public split yet between two camps forming across Silicon Valley. Meta, NVIDIA, Microsoft, and Elon Musk have been championing open, fast-moving AI. OpenAI and Anthropic, the same companies racing hardest to build the most powerful models, are now the ones publicly asking regulators to install brakes. If you work anywhere AI touches your job (so, everywhere), this argument over speed vs. caution is about to shape the actual rules you operate under, not just the headlines you scroll past.

**Our take:** Here's the detail nobody's talking about: Meta's own chief AI scientist, [Shengjia Zhao](https://fortune.com/2026/07/29/anthropic-deepmind-openai-meta-washington-ai-slowdown-plan/), _personally_ signed the pacing petition, the same one his boss just spent an entire op-ed arguing against. Most corporate disagreements happen between rival CEOs. This one's happening under the same roof. Worth asking: when the people closest to the frontier keep hedging their own bosses' bets, is that a coincidence, or a signal? Anyway, Grant got around to publishing his full thoughts on this. [Check out his hot take on to pace or not to pace here](https://www.theneuron.ai/blog/ai-needs-a-release-schedule-not-a-brake-pedal/).

# 🎓 AI Skill of the Day: Supervise Your Browser Agent

Browser agents are getting useful, which means they are also getting just dangerous enough to need adult supervision. [Perplexity’s Personal Computer](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/971750/perplexity-personal-computer-windows-ai-agents) now works on Windows, where it can use local files, Microsoft 365, and the web; [Polar](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/29/perplexity-employee-who-worked-on-comet-launches-an-ai-browser-aimed-at-knowledge-work/) is pitching saved prompts and tab-aware agents for knowledge work; and [Google](https://www.engadget.com/2224915/google-lets-mad-users-talk-to-gemini-by-pressing-fn/) is letting Mac users call Gemini from any window with a long fn-key press.

The skill: treat a computer-use agent like an intern with temporary access, not a wizard with your house keys.

1. Give it one bounded job: research, summarize, draft, compare, or organize.

2. Name the apps or folders it may use.

3. Require approval before sending, deleting, buying, submitting, or changing files.

4. Ask for an action log so you can audit what happened.

```
Act as my supervised browser/computer-use assistant.
Goal: [describe the task]
Allowed sources/apps: [tabs, files, websites, or apps]
Do not send, delete, submit, purchase, install, or change files without asking first.
When you finish, give me:
1. What you checked
2. What you changed or drafted
3. What still needs human judgment
4. Any action that requires my approval
```
