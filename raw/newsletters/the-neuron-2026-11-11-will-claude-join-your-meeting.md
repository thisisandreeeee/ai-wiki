The [Ring Drone Cam](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/devices/ring-drone-cam-rover-cam-security-robot) costs $249.99, ships January 27, 2027, and sits out of sight with its camera off. When a Ring camera, sensor, or alarm goes off, it takes flight and visits preset patrol points, recording 2K video while it spins 360 degrees. You can also send it on scheduled routes, though schedules require a Ring Protect plan.

Ring also announced the Rover Cam, a $999.99 patrol robot for businesses that is open for preorder and ships December 16. It rolls to saved spots in a store, does a 360-degree scan, and raises a periscope mast to peek over counters and shelves. It also packs a siren, a light tower, and two-way audio. Amazon calls it a guard who never clocks out.

Both robots run on Ring's AI Pilot, which uses cameras and vision models to map your space into a floor plan, label the rooms, and suggest patrol points like side doors, windows, and long hallways. Then it steers around your furniture in real time. Two caveats. The Drone Cam still needs FCC authorization before Amazon can sell it, and Amazon admits the propellers are not quiet. _Your cat did not get a vote._

# 😸 Google, Anthropic, and OpenAI all shipped big new tools in one day

It's not 11/11 for no reason. The top three AI labs handed us a pile of treats within a day of each other, and we're excited to see what you build with them.

Here's what happened:

* Google launched [Gemini 4 Carbon](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/gemini-4-carbon-now-available/), its strongest model, built for long agent jobs like coding, research, and security work.

* Anthropic launched [Claude Meetings](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/10/anthropic-launches-claude-meetings-to-turn-workplace-discussions-into-plans-and-tasks/), Sheets, and Whiteboards in beta, plus Claude Data and interactive connectors.

* OpenAI launched [Businesses in ChatGPT](https://openai.com/index/introducing-businesses-chatgpt/) in beta, where companies claim a profile and add their own chatbot.

The detail worth repeating is Carbon's price. Developers pay $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens (chunks of text a model reads and writes) until November 30. Then it doubles to $4 and $20. _So test it now, while it's on sale._ Carbon also powers /swarm in Google Antigravity, where one prompt becomes a team of agents. Google reports #1 rankings on several coding and agent benchmarks, though the tests are its own or partner-run.

Claude's launch is an office-suite move. Meetings joins your Google Meet call as a visible participant, drafts the plan while people talk, and saves notes and action items afterward. It won't join calls with outside guests unless an admin approves, and it leaves if one shows up. _A meeting attendee who actually does the action items. Wild._

OpenAI aimed at a different crowd. Businesses can claim a ChatGPT listing, upload product feeds, and see aggregated counts of mentions and website clicks, but no private chats. Verified businesses can add a labeled Business Agent that answers questions using only what they supply. It starts with U.S. e-commerce and online-service companies.

**Why it matters: **When the top models get upgrades, the biggest change for most of us is where the upgrades show up. They now ship with apps around them, so the new stuff lands inside tools you already open. That also shifts work toward AI drafting first and humans reviewing, like Claude's live meeting plan or ChatGPT's shopper answers.

If you're a casual user, expect spreadsheets and timelines to start showing up in your usual AI app. Try one chore this week, like a budget tracker (Claude's tools need a paid plan). Power users get more, with agent teams like /swarm and connectors that open Adobe or Atlassian inside Claude. Your skill becomes routing work to the right agent and checking what comes back.

Through all of it, the human part stays big. Claude Meetings announces itself and Business Agents are labeled, so people know when AI is involved and still own the final call. _That's the part to watch as these tools spread, because someone still has to sign off on the draft._

# 🎓 AI Skill of the Day: Fix AI-Sounding Drafts With a Two-Pass Edit

Developer Dodaios released [STE80 Humanizer](https://github.com/Dodaios/ste80-humanizer), a free, open-source skill that fixes text that sounds like AI. Instead of sprinkling in typos and fake quirks, it treats most AI tells as structure problems, like vague nouns, padded transitions, and sentences doing four jobs at once.

It works in two passes. Pass 1 removes 26 common AI tells. Pass 2 rebuilds sentences about 80% of the way toward Simplified Technical English, a controlled style from aerospace maintenance manuals. Its rules include one instruction per sentence, active voice, descriptions of 25 words or fewer, and one topic per paragraph. Stopping at 80% relaxes the strict word list, so the draft still sounds like a person wrote it.

You can borrow the mechanism without installing anything.

1. Paste your draft into the prompt below. It forces short, active sentences and keeps every fact intact.

2. Fix the three sentences it flags as still carrying too many ideas.

3. Read the result aloud and put back any personality the edit flattened. _Clean is good. Hospital-hallway clean is not._

```
Rewrite this draft for plain-language clarity. Preserve every fact, number, name, qualification, and source claim. Use active voice where accurate, keep one main idea per sentence, replace vague abstractions with concrete actors and actions, and remove repeated conclusions or filler transitions. Do not add facts, jokes, or confidence. After the rewrite, list three sentences that still carry too many ideas.

[PASTE DRAFT]
```
One caveat from the creator. This suits explanatory writing. For fiction, speeches, and brand copy, the skill runs only Pass 1.
