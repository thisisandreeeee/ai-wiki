# Recursive

**Recursive** is Richard Socher’s AI-research company, framed in a Latent.Space interview around automating parts of AI research and eventually applying self-improving systems to science, engineering, and invention. The source reports a $4.65B seed round and early results in optimization and GPU-kernel work; these claims are attributed to the interview and are not independently validated here.

## Research thesis

Socher describes a “Eureka Machine” that would accept goals and environments, then improve the process of invention itself. Recursive’s nearer-term focus is narrower: automate ideation, implementation, evaluation, and harness optimization for AI research. The interview distinguishes this from unbounded runaway self-improvement: hardware, physical, economic, and human adoption constraints still limit how quickly improvements can propagate. [raw/newsletters/latent-space-2026-09-14-why-you-should-work-on-ai-for-ai-research-richard-socher-of-recursive.md]

The company’s reported ingredients include open-endedness, evolutionary search, sandboxing, web search, reward engineering, and verification. The useful engineering question is whether an agent improves not only model code but also the search strategy, data-generation process, evaluation harness, and feedback loop.

## Governance implications

The interview is explicitly optimistic about AI for science while warning that reward hacking, weak objectives, and inadequate red teaming can turn “make the metric go up” into harmful behavior. Its arguments for application-specific regulation and open models sit in tension with the need for strong controls around autonomous research.
