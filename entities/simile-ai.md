# Simile AI

**Simile AI** is a simulation company founded by Joon Sung Park, whose work extends the *Generative Agents* / Smallville line into behavioral foundation models and digital twins.

## Simulation thesis

Latent.Space described Simile's approach as combining long-form interviews, observational and transaction data, and randomized controlled trials to model both what people say and the causal mechanisms behind what they do. In a study of 1,000 people, digital twins reportedly reproduced participants' behavior and attitudes at about 85% of the rate at which people reproduced their own responses. [raw/newsletters/latent-space-2026-08-21-simulation-the-new-scaling-law-joon-sung-park-simile-ai.md]

The product goal is not only to predict an outcome. A useful simulation explores the path from an intervention to a desired future, including counterintuitive intermediate steps, so teams can test products, policies, and messaging before deploying them. [[real-world-agent-evaluations]] supplies the evaluation mindset; [[self-driving-labs]] supplies the closed-loop experimental analogy.

## Why it matters

AINews framed simulation as a possible new scaling axis: synthetic populations can be cheaper and faster than repeated human panels, while still requiring grounding against real behavior. Simile's reported $2B Series B and Fortune 100 work make the idea commercially consequential, but accuracy claims remain source-reported and domain-dependent. [raw/newsletters/ainews-2026-08-22-ainews-10-worse-100x-cheaper-10000x-faster-why-simulation-is-taking-ov.md]

The technical boundary is important. Prompting a general LLM may reproduce rational or stereotyped answers, while modeling “social physics” may require behavior-specific data and post-training. That places simulation between [[agent-memory]], [[ai-benchmarking]], and causal experimentation rather than treating it as another chatbot wrapper.
