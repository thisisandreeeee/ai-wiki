# AI in Education

**AI in education** is the product, contract, and governance problem of introducing AI into schools while protecting student data, preserving human responsibility, and making classroom access configurable.

## November 2026 signals

Microsoft, the American Federation of Teachers, and the United Federation of Teachers announced an AI Safety & Privacy Standard that took effect on November 1. The reported contract terms prohibit training on student or educator data, selling or advertising against it, student tracking, and AI companions. They require human review for high-risk decisions, annual certification, audit rights, and breach reports within 72 hours, with cancellation, damages, or paused AI use available for violations. Any U.S. school district can add the standard to a Microsoft agreement without renegotiation. The standard governs the terms of use; it does not itself require schools to use AI. [[microsoft]] [[frontier-model-access-controls]] [raw/newsletters/the-neuron-2026-11-02-microsoft-s-binding-ai-school-deal.md]

Google announced controls that reportedly leave Gemini, Gemini Notebook, Gemini in Classroom, and Google Search off by default for K–12 student accounts until administrators enable them. A Guided Learning pilot is intended to walk students through homework step by step, and Google Classroom already lets teachers see chatbot transcripts. The important unresolved detail is whether schools can keep Gemini off while retaining Search, since the source describes the two as deeply integrated. ## Open questions

- Can contractual promises be audited and enforced consistently across products and subcontractors?
- Can schools disable generative features without losing basic search and accessibility tools?
- What data should teachers see for safeguarding, and how long should conversation transcripts remain available?
- How should districts validate learning outcomes rather than measure AI usage alone?
